OVERVIEW
--------
Dataset: 500 customers
Overall Churn Rate: 38.6% (193 churned, 307 retained)
Analysis Period: Last Month Activity Data

================================================================================
                         KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. MAJOR CHURN DRIVERS (Ranked by Importance)
----------------------------------------------

   a) TOTAL VIEWING HOURS (Importance: 24.2% - STRONGEST PREDICTOR)
      ✓ Churned customers: 66.6 hours/month (avg)
      ✓ Retained customers: 83.2 hours/month (avg)
      ✓ Difference: -20.0%
      → LOW ENGAGEMENT is the #1 predictor of churn
      → Customers watching <30 hours/month have highest churn risk

   b) AVERAGE SESSION DURATION (Importance: 19.8%)
      ✓ Churned customers: 49.4 minutes (avg)
      ✓ Retained customers: 57.8 minutes (avg)
      ✓ Difference: -14.4%
      → Shorter sessions indicate less engagement and higher churn risk

   c) NUMBER OF UNIQUE TITLES WATCHED (Importance: 16.5%)
      ✓ Churned customers: 19.5 titles/month (avg)
      ✓ Retained customers: 23.7 titles/month (avg)
      ✓ Difference: -18.1%
      → Less content variety correlates with higher churn

   d) CUSTOMER SERVICE INTERACTIONS (Importance: 11.7%)
      ✓ Churned customers: 3.2 interactions/year (avg)
      ✓ Retained customers: 2.5 interactions/year (avg)
      ✓ Difference: +27.7%
      → MORE customer service contacts = HIGHER churn risk
      → Suggests unresolved issues or dissatisfaction

   e) BINGE WATCHING SESSIONS (Importance: 10.8%)
      ✓ Churned customers: 6.2 sessions/month (avg)
      ✓ Retained customers: 7.7 sessions/month (avg)
      ✓ Difference: -19.7%
      → Binge watching behavior is protective against churn

================================================================================
                    SEGMENT-SPECIFIC INSIGHTS
================================================================================

2. CHURN BY SUBSCRIPTION TIER
------------------------------
   • Basic Tier: 43.5% churn rate (HIGHEST RISK)
     - 207 customers, 90 churned
     - Lower monthly cost ($7.99) but highest churn
   
   • Standard Tier: 39.5% churn rate (MODERATE RISK)
     - 210 customers, 83 churned
     - Mid-tier pricing ($12.99)
   
   • Premium Tier: 24.1% churn rate (LOWEST RISK)
     - 83 customers, 20 churned
     - Highest engagement despite higher cost ($17.99)
     - Almost HALF the churn rate of Basic tier

   → KEY INSIGHT: Premium subscribers are more loyal, suggesting that
     value perception and feature access reduce churn

3. CHURN BY CONTENT PREFERENCE (Top Genre)
------------------------------------------
   HIGHEST CHURN GENRES:
   • Horror: 52.3% churn rate (23 of 44 customers)
   • Thriller: 48.3% churn rate (14 of 29 customers)
   • Action: 44.6% churn rate (33 of 74 customers)
   • Romance: 41.8% churn rate (23 of 55 customers)

   LOWEST CHURN GENRES:
   • Documentary: 25.9% churn rate (14 of 54 customers)
   • Comedy: 33.0% churn rate (33 of 100 customers)
   • Drama: 35.3% churn rate (36 of 102 customers)

   → KEY INSIGHT: Documentary and comedy fans are more loyal
   → Horror/Thriller fans may have more niche preferences and
     higher likelihood to churn if content library is insufficient

4. VIEWING HOURS ANALYSIS
--------------------------
   Churn Rate by Viewing Hours Range:
   • 0-30 hours: HIGHEST CHURN RISK
   • 30-60 hours: HIGH CHURN RISK
   • 60-90 hours: MODERATE RISK
   • 90+ hours: LOWEST CHURN RISK

   → Critical threshold appears to be around 60-70 hours/month
   → Customers below this threshold should be prioritized for retention

================================================================================
                    STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:

1. ENGAGEMENT INTERVENTIONS (Target: Low viewing hours customers)
   • Identify customers with <50 hours/month viewing
   • Send personalized content recommendations
   • Implement "nudge" campaigns to increase engagement

2. CUSTOMER SERVICE QUALITY IMPROVEMENT
   • Customers with 3+ service interactions are HIGH RISK
   • Implement proactive follow-up after service contacts
   • Investigate root causes of service issues
   • Set up early warning system for repeat contacts

3. BASIC TIER RETENTION PROGRAM
   • Basic tier has 43.5% churn vs 24.1% for Premium
   • Consider:
     - Highlight upgrade benefits to Basic subscribers
     - Offer limited-time Premium features to Basic users
     - Improve Basic tier value proposition

4. CONTENT STRATEGY
   • Expand Documentary and Comedy content (low churn genres)
   • Improve Horror/Thriller catalog quality or variety
   • Promote binge-worthy series to increase session counts

5. PREDICTIVE CHURN SCORING
   • Deploy churn risk score for all customers
   • High-risk criteria:
     * <50 viewing hours/month
     * <6 binge sessions/month
     * 3+ customer service interactions
     * Basic tier subscription
     * Horror/Thriller primary genre

6. RETENTION CAMPAIGNS
   • Target customers showing early warning signs:
     - Declining viewing hours month-over-month
     - Decreasing session duration
     - Recent customer service contact
   • Offer targeted incentives before they decide to leave

================================================================================
                         CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The analysis reveals that ENGAGEMENT is the primary driver of retention.
Customers who watch more content, for longer sessions, with more variety,
and engage in binge-watching are significantly less likely to churn.

The most actionable insight is the strong correlation between customer
service interactions and churn (+27.7% higher for churned customers),
suggesting that many churning customers had unresolved issues.

Focusing retention efforts on:
   1. Low-engagement users (especially <50 hours/month)
   2. Basic tier subscribers (43.5% churn rate)
   3. Customers with recent customer service contacts

...should yield the highest impact on reducing overall churn rate.
