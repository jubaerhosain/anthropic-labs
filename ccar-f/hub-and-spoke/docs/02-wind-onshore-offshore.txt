INTERNAL TECHNOLOGY BRIEF - WIND GENERATION
Revision 6

Onshore.
Onshore wind LCOE averaged USD 0.034/kWh globally in 2024, making it the cheapest new-build
generation in most markets. Turbine nameplate ratings for new onshore orders cluster at
5-7 MW with rotor diameters above 160 m. Capacity factors of 35-45% are routine at good
sites; repowering older sub-2 MW fleets typically doubles output from the same footprint.

Offshore, fixed-bottom.
Fixed-bottom offshore reached USD 0.075/kWh in 2024. The 2023-2024 period saw material
cost stress: several US and UK projects were cancelled or renegotiated after monopile,
vessel and interest-rate inflation outran contracted strike prices. Turbine ratings of
14-16 MW are now standard for new orders, with 18-20 MW prototypes under test.

Offshore, floating.
Floating platforms unlock water depths beyond 60 m, which is most of the resource off Japan,
California and the Atlantic coast of Iberia. Cumulative deployment remains near 0.3 GW -
pilot scale. Semi-submersible designs dominate; mooring and dynamic-cable reliability are the
open engineering questions. Cost is currently 2-3x fixed-bottom and expected to converge
slowly through the 2030s.

Integration note.
Wind output is anticorrelated with solar on daily and seasonal timescales in most temperate
grids, which is why hybrid procurement lowers firming cost. See the grid integration file.
