INTERNAL TECHNOLOGY BRIEF - GEOTHERMAL
Revision 3

Conventional hydrothermal.
Global installed geothermal capacity is approximately 16 GW, concentrated in the US,
Indonesia, the Philippines, Turkey and Kenya. Capacity factors are 70-90% - the highest of
any renewable source - which makes geothermal a genuine baseload and firming resource rather
than an energy-only one. Growth has been slow, roughly 3% annually, because conventional
plants require a rare combination of heat, permeability and fluid.

Enhanced geothermal systems (EGS).
EGS engineers permeability into hot dry rock, removing the resource-rarity constraint and in
principle making geothermal siteable almost anywhere. Fervo Energy's Cape Station project in
Utah demonstrated 3.5 MW per well flow rates in 2024 and has contracted offtake; drilling
costs have fallen sharply by importing horizontal drilling and fibre-optic sensing practice
directly from shale. Induced seismicity remains the principal siting and permitting risk -
the 2017 Pohang event in South Korea is the reference case.

Superhot rock and closed-loop.
Superhot rock (>375 C, supercritical fluid) offers order-of-magnitude energy density gains
but has no field demonstration at power scale. Closed-loop designs (Eavor) avoid the
seismicity and fluid-loss problems at the cost of lower heat-transfer rates.

Direct use.
Roughly 175 GWth of direct-use geothermal for district heating, greenhouses and aquaculture
is installed globally - larger than the power sector and routinely omitted from renewable
electricity statistics.
