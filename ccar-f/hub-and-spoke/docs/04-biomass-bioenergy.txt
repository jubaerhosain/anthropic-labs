INTERNAL TECHNOLOGY BRIEF - BIOMASS AND BIOENERGY
Revision 5

Scale and forms.
Bioenergy supplies roughly 55% of global renewable final energy consumption when traditional
biomass for cooking and heating is counted - far more than wind and solar combined, and
routinely underweighted in reporting that looks only at electricity. Modern bioenergy splits
into solid biomass power, biogas and biomethane, liquid biofuels, and traditional use.

Carbon accounting.
The dominant controversy. Combustion emits CO2 at the stack at rates comparable to or above
coal per unit energy; the claimed neutrality depends entirely on regrowth over a payback
period that ranges from under 5 years for agricultural residues to 40-100 years for whole
mature trees. The EU's treatment of imported wood pellets as zero-carbon at the point of
combustion is the most-litigated instance of this accounting choice.

BECCS.
Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage is the negative-emissions pathway most heavily
relied upon in IPCC mitigation scenarios. Stockholm Exergi's Varta project reached FID in
2024 for roughly 800,000 tonnes CO2 per year. Sustainable feedstock supply, not capture
technology, is the binding constraint on scale.

Biogas and biomethane.
Anaerobic digestion of manure, food waste and sewage produces biogas upgradeable to grid-
injectable biomethane. Europe produced approximately 4.9 bcm of biomethane in 2024. The
methane-avoidance credit from diverting manure often exceeds the fuel's own combustion
emissions, which makes it one of the few unambiguously net-negative bioenergy routes.

Aviation fuel.
HEFA from waste oils is the only SAF pathway at commercial scale today and is feedstock-
limited. Alcohol-to-jet and gasification routes are pre-commercial.
