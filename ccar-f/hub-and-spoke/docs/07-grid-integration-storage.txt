INTERNAL TECHNOLOGY BRIEF - GRID INTEGRATION, STORAGE AND FIRMING
Revision 7

The integration problem.
Variable renewable penetration above roughly 30-40% of annual energy shifts the binding
constraint from generation cost to system flexibility. Curtailment, negative price hours and
the steep evening net-load ramp ("duck curve") are the observable symptoms. California
curtailed approximately 3.4 TWh of solar in 2024.

Battery storage.
Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) now dominates grid storage on cost and cycle life. Pack prices
fell below USD 100/kWh in 2024. Four-hour duration is the commercial standard; this covers
the daily solar shift but not multi-day wind lulls.

Long-duration storage.
The 10-100 hour regime is unsolved commercially. Candidates: pumped hydro (mature, 95% of
installed storage energy globally, but siting-constrained), compressed air, iron-air
(Form Energy's 100-hour chemistry, first installations 2024-2025), and thermal storage.

Firm low-carbon resources.
Geothermal, hydropower with reservoir, BECCS and hydrogen turbines can provide firming
without storage. Systems studies consistently find that the last 10-20% of decarbonisation
is far cheaper with a firm resource in the mix than with storage alone - which is the main
argument for keeping geothermal, biomass and dispatchable marine options in the portfolio
rather than optimising purely for lowest-LCOE variable generation.

Hydropower.
Approximately 1,400 GW installed globally, the largest renewable source of electricity.
Growth is limited by remaining sites, ecological objection and, increasingly, drought risk to
existing reservoir output.
